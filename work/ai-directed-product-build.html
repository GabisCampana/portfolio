Context

Fully specified, and not being built.

I had a product I believed in: a validated thesis, a written requirements doc, and a finished design handoff for ten screens. What I didn't have was an engineer. Hiring one was never the plan for this: the economics don't work at validation stage.

This is the position a lot of services firms are in with AI in delivery. They can see the work that should be automatable. They assume the only way to act on it is to hire engineers or buy a platform, so nothing happens.

The Approach

A tight written scope, not a blank check

I added a build specialist to my own operating system under a written contract: translate an already-final spec into working software, flag any deviation instead of quietly absorbing it, ship one flow at a time with a real on-device demo before starting the next, and route every product or scope decision back to me. No product, scope, or domain calls belong to the specialist. That line never moves.

  • Spec-to-code translation only: no product design, no research
  • Every deviation from spec explicitly flagged, never absorbed silently
  • One user flow shipped at a time, each demoed on a real device before the next starts
  • Every product, scope, and domain decision stays with me
The Result

Every flow built and demoable, except one still in progress

Every flow is built and demoable end to end except one (still in progress), including the hardest feature on the spec: an on-device visual assessment feature, which is fully functional. My own hands-on time stayed under five hours, for a build I estimate would have cost a freelance developer a minimum of $10,000 for design and MVP alone, and still wouldn't have been ready inside a month.

  • All ten screens specified and built, one flow still in progress
  • The hardest feature on the spec fully functional, not simplified to hit a date
  • Under five hours of my own hands-on time across the whole build
  • Estimated $10,000+ and over a month, had it gone to a freelance developer instead
